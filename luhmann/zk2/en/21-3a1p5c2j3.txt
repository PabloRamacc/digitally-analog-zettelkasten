The degree of sincerity or cynicism that someone achieves probably depends on the ability to comprehend the otherness of others.
In this sense, a typical characteristic of the sincere person is that he identifies his audience with himself; or as Goffman (p. 49 → B739) (pp. 80f. → B814) [*] says, → 21/3a1p5c2j3a makes himself into his own audience and then cannot gain the distance from his self-presentation which is a prerequisite for all objectivity and insight into other possibilities.
Furthermore, this reinterpretation of the concept of sincerity reveals how the ethical interpretation of human action is connected to the ontological conception of reality and loses its meaning with its collapse. It poses a terminological problem whether such terms that have served as designations for virtues should be retained at all if their meaning shifts so greatly through a change in the frame of reference.
