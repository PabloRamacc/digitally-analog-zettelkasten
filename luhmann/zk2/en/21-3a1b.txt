If the condition were replaceable, it would not be a genuine condition at all. Thus, tea is not an existential prerequisite of human life, and therefore the need for tea constitutes a poorly formulated functional requisite. In contrast to this stands the non-replaceable need for food. Conversely, if there were no such thing as food, then nourishment itself would cease to be a functional performance in any genuine sense, but rather merely one class of such performances (i.e., a variable in the sense of → 21/3a2). Consequently, existential prerequisites can only be reformulated into variables corresponding to respective performances. However, this still does not constitute an epistemic achievement; it remains nothing more than a tautology as long as the variable is not independent → 21/3a1c.
