An important possibility of distinction arises from the question to what extent differentiation extends. One should not immediately equate differentiation with subsystem formation.
Smelser (1959), p. 10ff. → B1192 distinguishes three levels of units in social systems and accordingly possibilities for social differentiation: a.) sets of activities;
b.) functions;
c.) organizations or collectivities composed of sub-roles.
Perhaps one could also draw on Parsons' distinction of values, norms, collectivities and roles (cf. → 21/3d5b16a), through which social differentiation at the same time could be graded in terms of a hierarchy of generalization (or specification). → 21/3d5b11e1a Then to that → 21/3d5b11e2
