→ 21/3a1h6 Here lies the characteristic difficulty: that the continued existence of a system can practically hardly ever be called into question, let alone experimentally risked; cf. → 21/3a1f1e. How then is one to empirically ascertain with certainty which are the critical conditions for its continuation?
This question touches upon—somewhat ambiguously—also Blau 1955, p. 11f. → B326: "… if social [S. 12] needs were defined as prerequisites for survival, as they often are, it could be impossible to speak of needs that have not been met in an enduring social system. The advantage of the more limited concept adopted here [cf. → 21/3a1k2e ] is that it permits the empirical testing of functional hypotheses even when no information about extinct social systems is available." – but this is only a spurious success. It refers solely to secondary functions, whose existence also does not depend on survival-models.
