Expectations presuppose the self-identification of the experiencing (expecting) personality and the identification with things in the world. They mediate between both identifications by making each accessible to the other. Expectations have a temporal dimension and are, even so, always consciously experience-related; they can be thematized as experiences. At the same time, however, they thematize thing-like possibilities in the world.
This dual structure entails that expectations consist of two components: the world view and the personal relevance of the expected event. Accordingly, the problem of expectation strength splits into two components: probability and desirability of what is expected. Both strengths are relatively invariant against each other. → 21/3a1pe5(R)
