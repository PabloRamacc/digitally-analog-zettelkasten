The correct underlying idea of this theory is that, first and foremost, all possibilities for differentiation must be theoretically broken down so that one can assess to what extent an organization has actually made use of these possibilities for differentiation and to what extent it is content with vague, undifferentiated task groupings. However, this is also where the value of this theory largely ends. One can also formulate the purpose of this theory as follows: a distinction must be made between task structure and job structure, since a single job can encompass multiple tasks and multiple jobs can collectively fulfill a single task. This, incidentally, is the essence of the principle of the division of labor and the concept of the job.
