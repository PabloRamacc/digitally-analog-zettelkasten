[*] One of the most far-reaching consequences of the traditional, ontological definition of system through the concepts whole, part and interdependence is the __complete neglect of the problem of environment__. This deficiency is the main reason why we must choose a different concept of system that opens up a perspective toward the environment.
An interesting piece of evidence for this is Redfield's 1955 lecture → B34, which strives to bring system/environment problems into view in opposition to older functionalism, but can only see them as internal problems of a more encompassing system because it still proceeds from ontological thinking within the part-and-whole schema.
The same applies for Parsons.
