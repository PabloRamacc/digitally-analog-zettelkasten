Another aspect of the social dimension is that every system structure, insofar as it establishes a certain order, __also__—__by virtue of that order__—opens __the door to side goals, secondary goals, or even divergent goals for the individual__—and that the system must take precautions against this in order to ensure the necessary stability of its own structure.
E.g.: A teacher develops sexual interest in his or her students. Relatively unrestrained behavior at a party serves as a model for other situations. A typical case is one in which the system externalizes behavior as having no consequences, and consequences are then created there (Cavan 1966, p. 240ff. → B1477.
