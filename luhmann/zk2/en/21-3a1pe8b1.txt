Operative and non-operative definitions of expectations each have functional __and__ dysfunctional consequences for themselves. They are to be analyzed from the perspective of various functions. Operative definitions of expectation serve primarily action preparation, whereas non-operative ones serve the stabilization of the system of expectations and protection against disappointments; they simultaneously provide the framework for any explanations of disappointment.
A particularly important type of non-operative definition of expectation is __norms__; cf. → 5311/4b2 X.
2.) If expectations are liable to be disappointed—that is, if they are defined operatively—it does not yet follow that their legitimacy stands or falls with a disappointment. There is also generalization in this regard too; and the further it goes, the more resistant to disappointment can expectations become. In contrast to the → 21/3a1pe8b2
