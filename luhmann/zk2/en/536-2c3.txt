4.) Encounters are __nested__ with other systems, particularly those of a different nature, such as groups, organizations, and cultural communities. An abstract elaboration is lacking, particularly the idea that this nesting is based on the same action being claimed by different systems. However, it is clearly stated that the systemic problems of the encounter and of the social group to which the participants belong are not the same, so that conflicts may arise in which the tactful preservation of the group’s situational system causes disadvantages and vice versa (p. 8ff., esp. p. 13 → B1729).
A special type of nesting is that with other encounters. Within an encounter, the need for a side encounter that is incompatible with it may arise; see, e.g., pp. 18ff. → B1729: “… that persons ostensibly engaged in one encounter can simultaneously sustain an additional ‘subordinated’ one.
