Higher interdependence leads not only to acceleration and time pressure but also to a shift in the relevance of time horizons.
In highly interdependent systems the triggering cause of disturbances loses significance and instead attention is directed at consequences. The future gains relevance over the past. Problems can in any case no longer be solved where they arise.
