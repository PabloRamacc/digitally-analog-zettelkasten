The meaning of the survival principle lies in an assignment given by the old principle of stability through immobility and rigidity. Naturally, a certain continuity of identifiability must be preserved; otherwise one cannot speak of the survival of the same thing. This continuous identifiability is no longer understood as a substantial core of being (which contains the essential elements in contrast to accidental dualities) that does not change itself, but rather as a structure of a system that regulates the exchange of all details and whose existence constitutes precisely the functional reference problem from which the equivalence of other possibilities can be determined.
