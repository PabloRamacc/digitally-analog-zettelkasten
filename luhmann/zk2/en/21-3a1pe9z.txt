One is convinced of values precisely because counterarguments are absent to them—not because they could be substantiated. Substantiations inevitably become entangled in a kind of ring exchange of value claims. {1}
Substantiating becomes unavoidable when one engages with others who hold different views about the same object, leading to circular reasoning where each side merely reasserts its own values while dismissing opposing ones as invalid or irrational. This dynamic reveals that debates over moral principles often lack a shared foundation for resolution, instead perpetuating conflict through mutual incomprehension rather than genuine dialogue aimed
