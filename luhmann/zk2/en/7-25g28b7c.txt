Replacing or supplementing grades with assessments based on objectified requirements (learning objectives) is also fraught with problems, because this requires a much stricter and more detailed definition of the curricula, thus permeating everyday life with testable material and orienting it entirely toward that material.
For more on such experiences, see Kornadt 1975, pp. 132ff. → B2805, which she does not, however, consider definitive.
At any rate, a structural conflict is emerging between objectification and academic freedom—or, more precisely, the flexibility in shaping the teaching/learning process.
Consequence for Kornadt → B2805 but: a less radical condemnation of grading.
