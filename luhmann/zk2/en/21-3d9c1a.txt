→ 21/3d9c1 Cf. the analysis of the "sense of a sentence" by Gomperz 1929, p. 36ff. → B1673, where following Dilthey and the holistic concept of sense (cf. → 21/3d18d24a1), one operates with the traditional prejudice that the whole (the sentence) is more than the sum of its parts (the words), → 21/3d9c1aa although this example itself almost classically proves the opposite: The sense of a sentence is less than the sum of the senses of the words, because indeed the sentence performs precisely a selection from the possible sense of the words.
