The previous focus on expectation theory and communication theory is insufficient for studying organizations whose actions consist of communication. However, this narrow focus carries risks, → 536/1,2 Namely: 1.) It fails to do justice to other types of organizations or associations whose social action is not limited to communication. And, more seriously,
2.) It leads communication theory to neglect the important fact that communication is a specific type of interaction. → 536/1,1 As a result, insight into the general traits of social behavior is easily lost in favor of the specific characteristics of communication. Furthermore, this approach easily leads to an overestimation of the extent to which behavior is shaped by roles.
