This is based on a general thought of systems theory: that a system can make itself less susceptible to disturbances through internal differentiation because all disturbances affect only parts, thus only specific performances (perhaps indeed life-sustaining ones), and are therefore isolable and localizable.
In the autonomy of subsystems lies, in other words, a certain protection against disturbances breaking through from one functional circle into another. Thus it depends on the environmental situation (or on other functionally equivalent stabilization strategies) of a system how much autonomy it must grant its parts.
