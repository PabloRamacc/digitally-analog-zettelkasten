__On the Problem of Abstraction__
It can be regarded as a certain conclusion of modern scientific theory and basic research that the sciences do not deal directly with concrete things in the world—not with stones, flowers, mountains, and lakes, nor with living people, groups, or organizations in the sense of a cooperative group of people. There are no precise concepts for concrete things in the world. Science, however, deals with concepts whose application to the world is problematic.
One can reject this scientific approach, just as one can reject the industrial social order or mechanical technology. One can refuse to enter the 20th century.
