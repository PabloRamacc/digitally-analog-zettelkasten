[*] Another aspect of systems theory is the abolition of the principle causa aequat effectum. Through system organization it becomes possible to give large effects to a "small" input on part of the system (e.g., the system leadership), because this organizes that part in such a way as to actualize other, latent, contributing causes. See also → 21/6i4b3.
A theoretical formulation of this idea can be found with Bertalanffy, p. 150f. → B989, who refers to the distinction between conservation causality and stimulus causality by A. Mittasch, From Chemistry to Philosophy, Ulm 1948 → B1406.
