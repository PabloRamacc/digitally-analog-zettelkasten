An attempt at a scientific theory of administration can therefore, at best, be a discussion of the direction of such abstraction, not the question of whether or not to abstract. Such a discussion can only take place if the abstraction can be controlled in terms of its direction, its method, and the applicability of its results. This is only possible if its abstract nature is clearly defined. Every representation is abstract. Anyone who believes, for example, that an organization “consists” of “people” is not thinking of concrete people in all the unimaginable richness of their lives. They are not thinking of the secretaries’ red lips—unless, perhaps, they view them as a functional work of art, produced and maintained during working hours X.
