Even in the case of symbolic action, the symbol clearly has the function of an aid to identification. Action expresses a feeling that cannot otherwise be identified and is without form or functional purpose.
Therefore, symbolic action is particularly necessary in the case of a lack of object-fixed feelings, or more precisely, of possible objects for feeling, as is typical with "transition problems," when one loses an object for feeling, or when access to suitable objects for feeling is socially blocked.
