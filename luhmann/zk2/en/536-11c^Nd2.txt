An important way to differentiate these levels appears to be “double communication” through content and style. One conveys a specific content and, through stylistic cues, implies certain attitudes that underpin the content, e.g., normative or cognitive expectations (temporal); serious or playful opinions (factual); friendly or hostile attitudes (social).
Structures are learned through the ability to understand and accept such double communication. From them, one can deduce valid frameworks for communication and interaction sequences.
This learning condition is geared toward __simple__ social systems.
