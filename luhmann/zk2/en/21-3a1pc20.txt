Smelser 1963 → B658 places the concept of __generalization__ at the center of a theory of __collective behavior__ (fundamentally pp. 71ff. → B658).
Collective behavior is defined 'formally' as "an uninstitutionalized mobilization for action in order to modify one or more kinds of strains[*] on the basis of a generalized reconstitution of a component of action." (p. 71 → B658)
Generalization here refers to short-circuiting, overestimation of meaning, etc.—that is, essentially measured against scientific exactness: certain excessively generalized notions are translated directly—too immediately—into action: "The accompanying expectation is that the strain can be relieved by a direct application of a generalized component." (p. 71 → B658)
Collective behavior is thus a kind of problem-solving through generalization, which becomes probable when institutions fail.
