→ 21/3d7c3 Criticism must indeed also guard against the typical sociological misunderstanding here: attributing to rational sciences the character of an empirical action-system theory and then claiming they do not meet its requirements.
In essence, pure economic theory in the neoclassical mould did not offer a theory of an action system—even if it occasionally sought psychological reference problems in its approaches—but rather a theory of movements in value changes (changes in goods) (so explicitly Albert 1961, p. 442 → B1303). Its modern continuation is therefore to be found in decision logic, not in a sociological subsystem theory.
