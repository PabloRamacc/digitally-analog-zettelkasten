If one analyzes the course of an interaction—rather than, for example, a specific role—as a unit, the question of in which role—i.e., according to which generalized behavioral expectations—the participants are acting in each instance must be treated as problematic. See also → 536/5c . The following problems, among others, then arise: 1.) A multitude of possible role relationships that may be relevant __simultaneously__, → 536/6A Related to this: a.) The problem of clarifying the currently valid conception of roles;
b.) The problem of reaching a consensus on this;
c.) The problem of the function of a deliberately multiple role relationship or the maintenance of a deliberately ambiguous referential situation.
2.) Problems associated with role shifts during the course of the interaction. → 536/6B
