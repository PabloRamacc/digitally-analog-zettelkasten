The functional concept in the social sciences originates from biology and was developed there through lengthy methodological struggles. → 21/2b2(R) The stakes of these struggles lay in the dichotomy between teleological and causal-mechanical explanation: Teleological explanation had become unacceptable since purposes lost their capacity for truth and turned into subjective conceptions, while causal-mechanical explanation was inadequate to biological phenomena, especially the phenomenon of the constancy of a certain form-result on the basis of different causes and changing life conditions. To overcome this dilemma, the system-referential functional concept was developed (where one can naturally doubt whether it constitutes a genuine overcoming or whether the functional concept ultimately reduces itself back to a combination of causal-mechanical type).
