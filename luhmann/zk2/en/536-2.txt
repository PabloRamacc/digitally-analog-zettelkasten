The basis for interaction theory is the theory of social identification or representation. In this respect, it builds upon → 21/3a1p5 .
This theoretical approach is advocated by Strauss and Goffman. → 536/2,1 However, the justification for this has hardly been elaborated upon. It lies in the fact that this approach makes it possible to apply the analytical technique of the functional method to interactions (as social units). This is done in the following way:
The continuation of the interaction is regarded as problematic and susceptible to disruption. It presupposes that the interaction will continue in a manner consistent with the self-presentation of the participants; it therefore presupposes a careful handling of these inherently problematic self-presentations, as well as the institutionalization of techniques for restoring smooth relationships following disruptions. It is, therefore, a matter of applying the standard functional method to interactions as units of reference.
