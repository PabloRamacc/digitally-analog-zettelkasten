Incongruent perspectives are meant as __substantively__ diverging; they thereby necessarily raise consensus problems. At least the consensus for the new perspective is initially problematic and only partially achievable. Furthermore, the more systematically one handles this method, it becomes clear that multiple perspectives, multiple ordering contexts are possible and equally legitimate, and that all claim and find consensus, so that consensus is always only partially achievable. See also → 21/3a1p8a3.
This connects with the fact that claims of incongruent perspectives generally intrude into stabilized social identifications and therefore are perceived as inappropriate, tactless, immoral (in the sense worked out in more detail in → 21/3a1p5).
