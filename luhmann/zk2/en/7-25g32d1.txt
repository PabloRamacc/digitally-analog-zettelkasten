The development of the special education system is “catalyzed” by a structural problem in the general education system. It is only as this school system expands that children with learning disabilities—who must nevertheless be educated—become a problem.
This also explains why differentiation, as a historical process, does not follow the logical schema of primary and secondary divisions (decomposition), but rather forms, so to speak, a process-logical sequence of its own kind, in that it must first develop the catalytic conditions for further systemic developments.
