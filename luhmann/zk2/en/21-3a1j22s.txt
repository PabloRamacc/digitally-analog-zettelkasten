The observation of second-order – in distinction from first-order – can be explicated at a different attitude towards the problem of reference, i.e., to the operative unity of indication/distinction.
The observer of first order performs designation; he refers and presupposes that this requires a distinction (selection etc.).
The observer of second order __thematises__ (designates) the distinction itself and must therefore imply another distinction and so on.
