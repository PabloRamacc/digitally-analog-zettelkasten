The relevant psychological research, especially experimental research, can hardly be overlooked anymore – this is partly due to the central importance of the concept.
Nevertheless, there are few observations and experiments specifically designed from the perspective of verifying a particular expectation theory. So Stogdill, p. 83 → B321.
Research deals largely with how expected "rewards" affect learning as reinforcement of expectations. As a result, Stogdill summarizes on p. 88 → B321: "that it is not the absolute size of a reward, but its magnitude in comparison with a range or scale of possible rewards, that determines its value as a reinforcer." This leads to the problem of generalization.
