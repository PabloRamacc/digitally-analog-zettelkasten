An interesting foreshadowing of this discussion can be found in the natural law explanation of *ius eminens* as a right of necessity that could only be applied to secure the preservation of the state or its parts.
This topic and, especially the question of what constitutes a "part" of the State (major pars bei Gottfried Achenwall, Jus Naturae II, Göttingen, 7th ed., Göttingen 1774, p. 146 → B371) inevitably led down an incline. Ultimately, the demand for the common good sufficed.
For the entire development of *ius eminens*, also see Rolf Stödter, Öffentliche Entschädigung, Hamburg 1933, pp. 52ff. → B372
