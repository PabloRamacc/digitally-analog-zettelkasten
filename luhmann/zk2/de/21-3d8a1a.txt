→ 21/3d8a1 Vgl. dazu auch Gouldner 1960, S. 164 → B986: "Thus far reciprocity has been discussed as a mutually contingent exchange of benefits between two or more units, as if it were an 'all or none' matter. Once the problem is posed in this way, however, it is apparent that reciprocity is not merely present or absent __but is, instead, quantitatively variable__ – or may be treated as such. The benefits exchanged, at one extreme, may be identical or equal. At the other logical extreme, one party may give nothing in return for the benefits it has received." usw.
