Interaction systems tend to reduce the perceptual load—that is, the need for reciprocal information—according to Jones and Thibaut (1958, p. 152) → B1734.
This could explain: 1.) the importance of firmly institutionalized social roles (Gehlen’s relief);
2.) the importance of personal familiarity in facilitating the continuation of interactions.
Reducing the __current__ perceptual load increases the potential for action, and perhaps also the freedom to act.
