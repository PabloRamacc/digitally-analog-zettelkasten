The theory of system connection is the formal aspect of the theory of social order → 21/3d8.
For action systems it is characteristic that they are stabilized not by causal-scientific laws – invariant relations –, but by expectations (constant orientations). The stability of a relation between two systems therefore also does not rest on the undisturbedness of an equilibrium of counteracting causes, but rather on the stability of complementary expectations.
Both in the natural sciences and in action sciences systems are causally connected, i.e., they act upon each other. It is not with regard to the assumption of causality as such that a difference exists, but rather with regard to the stabilization of causal relations through invariant-setting and isolation on one hand, and shared expectations on the other. → 21/3d8,3
