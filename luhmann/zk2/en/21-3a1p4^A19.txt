__Symbolic Action__
To be distinguished from the use of symbols in action (or in the constitution of the experience of acting) is __the employment of an act as a symbol__. This represents a special case within general symbolic theory, comparable to language on the same level.
A symbolic act occurs when the action itself assumes the function of a symbol: placing intended meaning into the world and making it visible. The action then becomes subject not to immediate causal efficacy but to this function; shaped by it, valued for its sake, and—viewed causally—is mostly meaningless.
As such symbolic acts are interpreted, rituals and ceremonial practices serve as examples. → 21/3a1p4A19a See also → 34/4d. → 21/3a1p4A19(R)
