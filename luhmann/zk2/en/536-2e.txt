The question of the direction of attention toward others.
It is a sound hypothesis to assume that, in basic interactions, one must pay attention to the other person as a whole, and that specialized forms of attention—such as focusing only on the nose, the shoes, etc.—always pose a problem.
Examples: 1.) The problem in service situations; 2.) The problem with people who look unusual, where attention is almost automatically drawn to what is unusual. See F. Davis 1961, esp. p. 123 → B811.
