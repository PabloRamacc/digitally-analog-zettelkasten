→ 21/3d5b4 See also the summary of Parsons 1961a, p. 325 → B1222, which more clearly highlights what is essential for Parsons: namely that the concept of system follows from complementarity or the abstraction of subjective action perspectives: pp. 324f. → B1222: "in the terminology I have used, each unit is both an actor and a __social object__. There are thus not categories of concrete entities, but analytically distinguishable aspects of the same entities. This is the concept of system (Black, 7 → B1223) which is of direct interest to the theory of action; that is, a system is seen as two or more interacting units which are at the same actors and social objects to each other."
System would then be the objectivation resulting from a plurality of actors, which overcomes the relativity of respective subjective perspectives. → 21/3d5b4,2
