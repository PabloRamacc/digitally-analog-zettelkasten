This thought can be linked to Parsons's further thesis that a system must solve four different and mutually contradictory basic problems, and that under certain environmental conditions it is best to resolve this contradiction by forming a special subsystem for each problem, so that the contradiction is thereby shifted into relationships between these subsystems. This mobilizes new solution possibilities (conflicts, exchange relations) for this problem. The inherent contradictoriness of system problems is the engine of differentiation.
