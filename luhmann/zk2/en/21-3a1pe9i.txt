Values / Self-Presentation
One can speak abstractly and conditionally about values, or refer to effects on values.
Something else is it when one commits oneself to values in action or judgment, in the sense of a self-presentation. Evaluations have precisely because they are not truth-apt a high significance for self-presentation. They do not understand themselves from themselves; their choice is personally attributed.
See also the distinction between "values" and "referring to values" with Rickert, *Cultural Science and Natural Science*, 2nd edition 1910, p. 90ff., 145 → B715: valuing is praising and blaming; referring to values does not do so.
This distinction becomes complicated because even the restraint in value questions contains a form of self-presentation – precisely because evaluating is one such thing.
