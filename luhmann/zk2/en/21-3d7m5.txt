A central moment of system/environment relations lies in the principle of functional equivalence, which is precisely why it is not a purely scientific principle but an organizational principle anchored within systems themselves.
A theory that accentuates this aspect is Egon Brunswik's psychology; cf. → 52/5,1a.
One might perhaps say: functional equivalences are a __compensatory means for the complexity gap between system and environment.__
The system/environment problem requires __structural__ provision for substitution possibilities.
From this, difficulties regarding quantification may arise; cf. → 21/6jb.
