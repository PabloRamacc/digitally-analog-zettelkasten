It may be that this transformation has already occurred in the heat of the conflict previous to the intervention of lawyers and courts, thus making it insoluble through compromise. But if the transformation has not already occurred it must be effected before the parties can enter into effective litigation. When a conflict turns into a lawsuit and litigation, something else is [*] bound to happen: The basic interaction situation changes from a dyad to a triad. Between these two transformations there exists some kind of functional relationship. […] The clash of interests is from now on formulated as a disagreement concerning either certain facts in the past or concerning what norms apply to the existing state of affairs, or both, […]. The needs of the parties, their wishes for the future, cease to [S. 99] be relevant to the solution. → 21/3d8c17c2
