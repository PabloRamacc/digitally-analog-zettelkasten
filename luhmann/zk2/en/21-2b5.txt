Another important limitation of analogy mentioned by Radcliffe-Brown himself → B270: biological organisms are typologically fixed: "an animal organism does not, in the course of its life, change its structural type. A pig does not become a hippopotamus. […] On the other hand a society in the course of its history can and does change in structural type without any breach of continuity [p. 397 → B270]."
Furthermore, Radcliffe-Brown (1935, p. 398) → B270 also mentions the lack of the criterion of death from the perspective that with it a clear-cut criterion for social pathology – for recognizing and separating out dysfunctions is missing.
In its place, Radcliffe-Brown (1935, pp. 397f.) → B270 sets up the notion of a "functional unity of the social system".
