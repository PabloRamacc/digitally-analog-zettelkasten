A means between solvable and unsolvable problems is the ill-defined problem in the sense of Reitman (1964) → B541.
Ill-defined problems mean, among other things: 1.) that the problem can be modified during the elaboration of a solution to adapt it to work results, happy insights, or achievable solutions (Reitman, especially p. 307ff. → B541);
2.) that consensus on the duality of a solution as a solution is not secured in advance.
