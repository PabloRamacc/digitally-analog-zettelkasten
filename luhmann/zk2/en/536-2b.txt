Actual interactions are __immensely complex__ if one wishes to analyze them in their full concreteness, in all their imagined and unimaginable relationships. This applies in all three dimensions: in their ongoing development, in the participation of visible and invisible actors (reference group!), and in the substantive complexity of the topics communicated. Strauss, in 1959, pp. 54ff., addressed this complexity of the actual course of interaction, which goes far beyond the concepts of conventional social science theories. → B666 emphasized.
The functional method offers a way to reduce this complexity—from the perspective of the problem of the continuation of interactions—to structures that can be addressed theoretically.
