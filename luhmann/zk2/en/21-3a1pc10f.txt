A striking weakness of the behaviorist stimulus or response generalization theory is that it essentially presupposes generalization in the environment as "similarity," and thus arrives at purely tautological "laws.
See e.g. Stendenbach 1964, p. 71 → B643: "The more a situation resembles earlier[*] ones in which the action of A was reinforced, the greater is the probability that A will behave as it did in those earlier situations (stimulus generalization).
Similarly Mowrer 1960, p. 26ff. → B648; better probably the same author, *Learning Theory and Behavior*, New York 1960, Chap. 12 → B649
