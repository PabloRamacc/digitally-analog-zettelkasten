If this is correct, then it makes sense that there is one basic dilemma concerning time-allocation and another regarding 'space,' i.e., the coexistence of units and systems, if we understand by this that these two problems are not reducible to each other: organization of action within units in systems cannot be reduced to either. Given a particular unit of organizational action, an actor, no level of optimization of the distribution of gratifications over time, however high, will on its own solve the problems of his relations with other units. Similarly, no solution to the problem of integration with other units in a system or adaptation to objects outside the system will by itself resolve his time-allocation problem. This is the sense in which we conceive these two basic dilemmas as independent of each other." (Parsons 1953, p. 623-624 → B1177)
