For a radical critique of the concept of systems using the schema of parts and the whole, see Beer 1961, p. 13ff. → B68 (“On Myths of Parts and Wholes”), not so much from the perspective that parts cannot simply be added together, but precisely in the central thesis of this myth: what meaning these concepts can have if the whole is even conceived of as divisible at all.
Essentially, this criticism amounts to a critique of specialization. It points to the necessity of preserving a degree of indeterminacy (variety) in the structure. For this reason, the ideal system model is said to be the human brain.
See also Beer 1962, p. 24 → B69 with the definition of a system as: “any collection of interrelated parts.”
