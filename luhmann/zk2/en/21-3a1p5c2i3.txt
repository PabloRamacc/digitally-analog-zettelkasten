For justification of the possibility of deception or "misrepresentation" in the use of symbols (as the sole means of communication of identifications), see also Goffman, p. 37ff. → B739 (p. 58 → B814), 162; p. 37 → B739 (p. 58 → B814)[*] → B814:
"It was suggested earlier that an audience is able to orient itself in a situation by accepting performed cues on faith, treating these signs as evidence of something greater than or different from the sign-vehicles themselves. If this tendency of the audience to accept signs places the performer in a position to be misunderstood and makes it necessary for him to exercise expressive care regarding everything he does when before the audience, so also this sign-accepting tendency puts the audience in a position to be duped and misled, for there are few signs that cannot be used to attest to the presence of something that is not really there." → 21/3a1p5c2i4
