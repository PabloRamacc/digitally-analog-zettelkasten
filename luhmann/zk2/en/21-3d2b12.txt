The implications of the means-end schema (i.e., the definition of actions in terms of means and ends) on interdependence must be considered, because possibilities for a rational variation of the degree of dependence could be hidden therein.
Cottrell 1963, pp. 249ff. → B1150 distinguishes in the wake of E.J. Thomas, *Effects of facilitative role interdependence and individual ego strength on group functions*,[*] Human Relations 10 (1957), pp. 347–66 → B1151 between goal interdependence and means-interdependence. The difference is not clear; because there purpose and means depend on each other as well, one dependence must imply the other. Perhaps the intended distinction refers to competitive versus cooperative situations, that is, whether others endanger one's own means or serve them as one's own means.
