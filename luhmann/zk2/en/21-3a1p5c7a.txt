This includes above all the fact that one speaks differently about a person when they are absent than when present. See also → 21/3a1p5c4fB1e; Goffman, pp. 108-112 → B739. This difference is formalized for example in rules regarding the use of titles etc. in direct address or when speaking about ….
Such communications have a special appeal when they take place in the presence of an audience but without being noticed by it, even though they presuppose absence; precisely then the riskiness of the situation increases their effect: secret exchange of glances or gestures, ambiguous speech, allusions that the spectator does not understand, etc.
