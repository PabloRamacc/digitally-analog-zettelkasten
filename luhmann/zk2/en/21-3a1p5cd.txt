→ 21/3a1p5c Fundamentally, there is first the insight into the compelling need for consistency in social identifications, which in turn finds its justification from expectation theory; see also the "empirical" proof through the feeling of shame; cf. → 21/3a1p4A17b [*]. Cf. further → 3522/3a.
Consistency is primarily a requirement of the __social__ system; it serves to form fixed behavioral expectations directed at comprehensible persons. With self-presentation as consistent, the individual submits to this dictum.
Cf. also Berger 1963, p. 107ff. → B514 with remarks on this social as well as internal pressure toward consistency.
