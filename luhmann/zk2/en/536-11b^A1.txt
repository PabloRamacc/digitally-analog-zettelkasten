Distinguishing sociological explanation from an understanding that is sufficient within the lifeworld.
In lifeworld behavior, familiarity suffices as a basis for understanding—at least when it can be assumed to exist intersubjectively (i.e., to be mutually presupposed).
It is precisely this familiarity that initially hinders sociological analysis; the reduction of unfamiliar circumstances to familiar ones is satisfying and sufficient. Why make a new attempt?
