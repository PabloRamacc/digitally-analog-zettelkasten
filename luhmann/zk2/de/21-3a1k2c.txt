In der präzisen Beziehung auf bestimmte Bedürfnisse sieht Malinowski gerade den Vorteil seiner Art von Funktionalismus.
Vgl. 1944, S. 159 → B528: "Functionalism would not be so functional after all, unless it could define the concept of function not merely by such glib expressions as 'the contribution which a partial activity makes to the total activity of which it is a part,' but by a much more definite and concrete reference to what actually occurs and what can be observed. As we shall see, such a definition is provided by showing that human institutions, as well as partial activities within these, are related to primary, that is, biological, or derived, that is, cultural needs. Function means, therefore, always the satisfaction of a need …."
