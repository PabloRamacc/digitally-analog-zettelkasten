Behavior in interactions can be analyzed as the steering of states of consensus. → 536/4,2 ; → 536/4,3
Here, we must start from the fundamental principle to which the theory of formalization also refers: that consensus always conveys and symbolizes a sense of belonging. The directions in which interaction management reveals or obscures possibilities for consensus therefore simultaneously determine future possibilities for contact; in particular—when those present are involved—they also determine whether the interaction can continue.
