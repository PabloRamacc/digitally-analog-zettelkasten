The problematic nature of the concept of stock as a functional criterion lies in the fact that not everything can be changed at once. Every change presupposes fixed structures; cf. → 54/4 This means that the stock criterion becomes ambiguous and perhaps unusable as soon as it is applied to complex relations, especially systems (unless a clear definition is possible, such as through the criterion of death in biology). → 21/3d2eOne must then additionally specify which structures are to remain valid relative to which changes. Depending on which structures used for the definition of validity serve as the reference point, different functional analyses and other functions result.
This train of thought uses Nagel 1961, p. 529ff. → B253 Against the critique of functionalism: In contrast to older survival theories that too simply equate with biological analogies → 21/3d2e1
