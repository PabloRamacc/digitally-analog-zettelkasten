Coombs et al., p. 25ff. → B353It might be well here to draw clearly the distinction between a **model** and a **theory**. A **model** is not itself a **theory**; it is only an available or possible or potential **theory** until a segment of the real world has been mapped into it. Then the **model** becomes a **theory** about the real world. As a **theory**, it can be accepted or rejected on the basis of how well it works. As a **model**, it can only be right or wrong on logical [?] grounds. A **model** must satisfy only internal criteria; a **theory** must satisfy external criteria as well.
After this, a model is nothing else than an unfinished theory. The possibility of non-theoretical use of models is not considered.
