Tendencies towards secrecy within bureaucracy; cf. → 532/4e11f.
C.J. Friedrich (1946, p. 52 → B837) considers it "the natural tendency of officials to be secretive", but grants the right to partial secrecy in a carefully balanced presentation without further reasons.
Morstein Marx 1956, p. 367f. → B867: especially regarding the connection between bureaucratic secrecy and pretentiousness. On the other hand, the duty of confidentiality is also highlighted. Virtue and sin dwell side by side.
Vincent – Mayers 1959, p. 252ff. → B868: tendencies toward secrecy in organizations as elitist and undemocratic.
Schweitzer 1964, p. 43f. → B206: Secret bureaucracy treated as a single type of the general prototype bureaucracy—especially from the perspective of participation in political power thereby achieved.
