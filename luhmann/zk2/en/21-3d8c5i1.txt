See also A.K. Cohen 1965, p. 6, 12f → B830, who among other things critically highlights this aspect and points to the different approach of the Mead school and its basic idea of identification through self-presentation in roles. In this context, the means-end schema plays a subordinate role. A fusion of these two approaches is demanded – but not achieved. Fundamentally, the difference lies in the choice of different system references: social order (with internal tensions) in the first case, personality (with problems of self-presentation) in the second; but not in a fundamental critique of the means-end schema.
