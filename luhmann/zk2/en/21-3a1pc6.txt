__Temporal generalization of expectations__ → 21/3a1pc6(R)
See also → 23/3d; → 54/2c; → 54/2h; → 21/3d8c5d; → 31/3c11.
Every expectation implies a certain temporal conception regarding the point in time or period during which what is expected occurs. In this regard, an expectation can be generalized; that is, it may fix a specific point in time. If the event does not occur at this point __not__, the expectation is disappointed. A later occurrence constitutes another event and pertains to other expectations. For example, as with a rendezvous. Or it may leave open more or less of an interval during which the event can happen. However, the question cannot remain entirely open; one may be uncertain regarding whether something will occur __at all__. But one cannot expect anything with certainty without any temporal conception whatsoever.
