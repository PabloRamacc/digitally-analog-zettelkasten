the introduction of the concept of 'partial function'. If the main variable of a process – for example, blood sugar content or voltage in a conductor – remains constant over one certain time interval while fluctuating over another, we call this process – following Ashby – a partial function. Constancy means that the process maintains a specific value unchanged over the time interval, where this value can also be zero; in this case, the process is temporarily interrupted [...]. An adaptation can despite acquiring many other adaptations be preserved, and a structure alongside many others [*] maintained, because between individual subsystems or combinations of subsystems stands [p. 62] a wall of partial functions whose values do not change, which thus shield the effects of different behaviors on each other.
