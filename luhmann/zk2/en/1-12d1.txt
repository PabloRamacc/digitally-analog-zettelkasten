Only in purely descriptive, constatative science—which abandons the claim of being able to form direct conceptions of action in favor of a mere depiction of the lawful course of behavior—are forms of thought of an objective, i.e., primarily action-alienated nature available. If these are applied to human beings as objects of study, the primacy of consciousness[*] loses its hidden epistemological foundation, and the agent’s awareness of motives or conception of goals[*] of the agent will then often have to be regarded as a secondary, superficial, or dependent factor among others in the determination of the action.
