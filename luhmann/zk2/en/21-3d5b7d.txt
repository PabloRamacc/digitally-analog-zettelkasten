One of the most sensitive weaknesses of the concept of analytical subsystems lies in transposing the system concept into a merely imagined order with the consequence that even Input/Output categories must be represented as exchange relations of the analytical system. This leads Parsons to the thesis of a complex yet exhaustively graspable network of interchange relations.
Critically, one would have to raise objections against straining the system concept too far. It is conceivable that within the four sub-orders various diverging directions of generalization of communication can be demonstrated – as in my work on fundamental rights → B958[*] – but hardly concrete action systems. The Input/Output concepts should, if they are to denote concrete, verifiable events, be restricted to concrete action systems. See also → 21/3d7d15.
