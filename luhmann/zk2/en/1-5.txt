In the social sciences, the functional method is regarded as __a__ research method—specifically, as one approach to conceptualization and establishing relationships __among other things__. Some researchers have devoted themselves to it—as if it were a field of research—and have achieved a certain degree of success. Others reject it, accuse it of containing value implications, or deny its independence. The question of its empirical relevance and testability also remains quite open. Nevertheless, when consciously applied, it dominates a considerable portion of research, and the scope of its unclear, tacit application is far greater still.
Given this situation, the small number of methodological studies is surprising. Perhaps this is due to the general prestige of the concept of “function,” which everyone thinks they understand.
