The apparently paradoxical finding rests precisely on the possibility of anticipating disappointments. In a factual sense, expectations are then more stable, but their security function for personality suffers from such calculated uncertainty.
See Stogdill, p. 92 → B321, referring to L.G. Humphreys' experiment, Acquisition and Extinction of verbal expectations in a situation analogous to conditioning, J. exp. Psychol. 25 (1939), pp. 294-301 → B678. Secure expectations (established experimentally) were destroyed absolutely more quickly by repeated disappointment than insecure ones: "Inconsistent reinforcement is here shown to increase the resistance of expectation to extinction."
