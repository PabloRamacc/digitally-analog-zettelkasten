Beyond Parsons' remarks, the significance of the distinction instrumental/consumptiv must be further clarified in connection with the problem of time. This becomes particularly evident when one abandons anthropological language such as "needs" and their "satisfactions," and consistently understands time as a reduction of complexity. The distinction between expr and instrumental then represents a differentiation of modes of reducing complexity: one long-term, circuitous, self-still complex, and thus itself in need of reduction; the other short-term-drastic, symbolically-generalized, and therefore expressive.
