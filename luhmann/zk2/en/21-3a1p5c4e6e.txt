Collegiality is generally related to specific representation tasks and thus does not encompass the whole person. Furthermore, collegiality implies equality of status; this leads to difficult status problems when colleagues are unequal in other relationships, for example in the case of negro professionals in the USA; cf. Goffman, p. 104 → B739.
Gross 1958, pp. 222ff. → B883 fundamentally assumes that all colleagues perform the same work and derives from this forms of intensification of collegial relationships (e.g., esprit de corps, intake control, professional consciousness, informal contacts, competition control, professional organizations).
