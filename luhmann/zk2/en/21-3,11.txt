A main point of attack for critics of functionalism is the ambiguity in formulating the functional reference criteria → 21/3.
Evidence for this criticism:
Buckley, p. 243/44 → B354, citing Linton → B355 and Sorokin → B356 (ambiguity of the term "integration of culture", further pp. 252ff. → B354 – generally on problems in formulating functional requisites).
Functionalism responds with a refinement through the distinction between the system as a whole and individual reference points, cf. → 21/3a1h. However, this final solution can only be given by means of a methodological clarification of the concept of a reference point.
Nagel 1961, p. 526ff. → B253, who considers this problem solvable in biology but not in the social sciences. See also → 21/2b4.
Homans (1950), p. 268ff. → B91; Firth, p. 240 → B337 → 21/3,11(R) ; Homans 1964b, p. 963ff. → B300

→ 21/3,11 Further evidence: Wright, p. 617f. → B350 (who considers this problem solvable – essentially through the definition of what is regarded as a normal state; in Wright's → B350 special case: normal information deemed sufficient "to cope with the environment"). See also → 25/1l1.
Also very critical are Carlsson 1962 → B278, especially regarding the choice of society as the unit of reference. The question of death, etc., is discussed in the usual sense.
