Where do the decisive advantages of functional differentiation lie?
It enables decisions based on choices between comparable alternatives (i.e., functionally equivalent problem solutions), thereby facilitating rationalization. It simultaneously allows for abstraction and simplification of the decision-making process, thus yielding greater performance with equal attentional demand.
Stephen Toulmin 1964, p. 354 → B1279 calls this the "Chalk-and-Cheese Principle": namely, that the structure of departments and advisory committees should be so ordered that, at each point, decisions have to be taken between commensurable alternatives." A decision between chalk and cheese can only be made politically.
