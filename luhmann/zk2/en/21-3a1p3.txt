Every attempt to form a fulfillment- and consensus-safe, consistent order of expectations leads to sealing off a specific context of expectation against an environment. Here the inside/outside schema becomes relevant for the first time.
This means that the reinforcement of expectations itself, as it progresses and succeeds, increasingly immobilizes expectations and offers the possibility to bring about stability through changes in the expectations themselves. At least not in an isolated manner, but only in a systematic way. {1} {2} {3}
This necessarily gives rise to problems of adapting the relatively invariant system to a environment that is independent from and variable with respect to systemic requirements.
