Remarkable is the finding to which Rödig arrives in 1969, p. 65 → B1112, that "the systematization leading from facts to legal provisions begins with thinking in alternatives, and indeed with setting alternatives into one."
The basic juridical concepts of this systematics rely on the alternativity of factual situations, as Rödig → B1112 demonstrates through several central examples.
However, a more precise characterization remains open regarding how one arrives at the construction and limitation of alternatives.
(Parallels to Klug, Jur Logik3, p. 123 → B1131: leaving open the respective boundaries of the "similarity stimulus".)
