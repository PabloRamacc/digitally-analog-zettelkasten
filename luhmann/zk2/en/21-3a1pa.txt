That one must define the stock of an action system through stability of expectations does not lie in the fact that expectations are ontologically more fundamental than actions in any sense. Phenomenology is not an ontology of subjectivity. Rather, it lies in the fact that the problematics of expectation suits itself better as a starting point for functional analyses than the problematics of action, __because it makes more alternatives visible__. One can stabilize expectations from out the factuality of experienced actions, but also from norms and consensus. The regularity of actually-expected behavior is however only __one__ functional equivalent for the formation of stable expectation, which although not fully, yet to a certain extent, is replaceable.
