→ 21/3a1k __On the concept of problem__
__Hemphill, p. 89 → B506__: "A problem is here defined as a state of affairs that is perceived with dissatisfaction." Inapplicable because there are also problems in social processes that go unperceived.
Donald M. Johnson, The Psychology of Thought and Judgment, New York 1955, p. 63 → B507: "A person may be said to have a problem if he is motivated toward a goal and his first goal-directed response is unrewarding."
George Humphrey, Thinking, London 1951, p. 312 → B508: "A problem is a situation which for some reasons[*] appreciably holds up an organism in its effort[*] to reach a goal."
Both cited after Braybrooke / Lindblom , p. 54 → B509.
