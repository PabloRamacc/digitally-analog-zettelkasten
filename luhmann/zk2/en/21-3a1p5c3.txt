Social identities must be __symbolized__ in order to become experiential; they do not possess a perceptible natural form as such.
Symbol theory finds one of its most important applications here: symbols serve essentially for the direct or indirect communication of social identifications. See fundamentally → 21/3a1p4A on this, and also → 21/3a1p5c3(R).
__This means that social identifications become experiential through the active use of symbols__. The problematic constitution of social identities can therefore be formulated as a problematics of consistent symbol usage in action.
In this sense, analyses by Goffman, especially p. 13ff. → B739, can also be understood, although he does not make use of a systematically introduced theory of symbols.
