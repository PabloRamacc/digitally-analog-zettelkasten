The described trust mechanism can only develop where trust is needed for the absorption of complexity. Where a system can be programmed with regard to optimal results, there is no trust. In such cases, for example, the decision-making freedom of the one who grants trust and thus allows influence on the outcome by the one trusted is lacking; consequently, that person also lacks an incentive to compete for trust.
Therefore, business administration does not calculate with trust (although it is naturally indispensable in the factual world of enterprise – see Guest 1962 → B619).
