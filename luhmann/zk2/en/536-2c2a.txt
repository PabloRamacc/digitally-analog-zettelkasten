→ 536/2c2 The autonomy of the situational system and its systemic problems means that its maintenance may require actions that contradict the needs of broader systems; in other words, that have dysfunctional consequences in larger systems.
See Goffman 1961, p. 13 → B1729 on the difference “between being a member of a group and being a participant in a gathering”—in the sense of differing demands.
Throughout Goffman’s work → B1729 The criticism implies that the behavioral requirements in situational systems cannot be easily met without discrepancy based on roles in general social systems, but rather require a certain degree of “role distance.”
