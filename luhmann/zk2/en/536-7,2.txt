→ 536/7 What is the __sociological__ aspect (as opposed to the natural scientific, psychological, or social-psychological aspect of interaction) of a theory of simple social systems?
In my opinion, the problem-oriented approach: that it focuses on the function of social systems that relieves consciousness (by reducing complexity)—namely, what makes a social system a system.
This also explains well the peculiarly alienating, enlightening, and revealing nature of sociological inquiry.
For comparison, see Garfinkel’s 1967 ethnomethodology, which is related but more phenomenological and descriptive, and not as clearly problem-centered → B1864 as a form of questioning → 536/7,3
