The generalization of expectations has as its most important consequence __that expectations cannot always truly correspond to attitudes__, neither on the side of the expecter nor on the side of whose behavior is expected. In this lies a dysf consequence of generalization, which triggers a series of follow-up problems. In particular, representation problems arise from it: either one must additionally represent one's true attitude as conforming or deviating, or one must conceal it. Furthermore, motivation becomes problematic: there are pleasant and unpleasant duties.
