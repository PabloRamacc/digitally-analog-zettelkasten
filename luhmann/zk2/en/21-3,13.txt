→ 21/3 As particularly developed by Levy, pp. 27ff.; especially 46f.; 63 → B328, concrete action systems at various "levels of generalization" can also be thematized and taken as the unit of reference. One may focus on their most generalized typicality – such as society or group in itself – or increasingly accept concretizing individual features (industrial society, informal workgroup → American post-WWII society, etc.).
The chosen "level of generalization" is then also decisive for the relevant environmental segment and for the stock prerequisites. A change in the level of generalization alters these relationships and thus the entire analysis.
Furthermore, it is important that a perfect unit of reference is unattainable. Every scientific analysis must omit details; this is not peculiar to functional analysis alone. In Levy → B328, there lies no elaborated theory of abstraction → 21/3,14
