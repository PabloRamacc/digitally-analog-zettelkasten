A central thesis of problem-oriented research theory is the __permanence of problematicity__. Problems are __recurrent burdens on action__. Problem-solving does not eliminate problems but merely enables their ongoing absorption in action.
This conception applies – quite consciously – to legal problem thinking, explicitly e.g., by Viehweg 1953, p. 21 → B511.
The permanence of system problems stems from the permanence of certain (disordered) environmental conditions to which the system is exposed.
