Social progress is to be understood as progress toward a higher potential for complexity, and this entails an increasing replacement of segregating by functional differentiation.
This thought simultaneously enables a critique of the prevailing definition through differentiation: Differentiation is only one, albeit indispensable, mechanism for reducing high complexity; thus it is an accompanying consequence of high complexity, not the essence of the matter itself. Alongside this, functionally equivalent mechanisms of reduction must be considered, e.g., reflexivity. The prevailing structurally interpreting theory of evolution blocks the view on this fact.
