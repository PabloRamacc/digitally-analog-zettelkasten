For the history of the ontological concept of “part,” see Beer 1961, p. 14 → B68:
"Leibniz said that it __expresses__ the whole, and this was because he saw parts not as divisions of space or divisions of function but as __dynamically involved__ in the whole: a 'systems' outlook. Hegel went further: every relation by which terms are related is an integral part of the terms it relates. And Whitehead went even further by making Hegel’s indivisible but passive System of Relatedness __active__. His doctrine of “prehension” viewed things as shaping their own nature out of other things by seizing upon relationships with them.”
