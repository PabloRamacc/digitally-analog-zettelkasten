The stronger the differentiation, the more problematic the integration of the system.
This thesis, which stems from old notions of division of labor and coordination, is also found in modern differentiation theory. See e.g., Eisenstadt 1964b, p. 377f → B1284; already Durkheim, De la Division du Travail → B1302; Smelser 1963c → B1240. One must guard against equating integration too hastily with hierarchical centralization or even political rule – precisely because of the origin of this thought. There are other forms of integration, e.g., regulated separation itself. See also the ability for mutual interpretation of subsystems briefly mentioned by Eisenstadt 1964b, p. 377 → B1284.
