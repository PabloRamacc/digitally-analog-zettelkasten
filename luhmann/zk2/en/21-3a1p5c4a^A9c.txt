Idealizations as false yet nevertheless fruitful constructions of science.
Bühl 1974, p. 54 → B833 The theory of gases, for example, works with laws based on assumptions that everyone knows are "false," i.e., that they are realized and realizable nowhere in this form: the molecules are regarded as perfectly elastic, their mass is compressed into a point without volume, there are no intermolecular forces acting, etc.
