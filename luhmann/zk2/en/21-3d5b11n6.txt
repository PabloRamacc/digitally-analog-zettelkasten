Eisenstadt (1964b) → B1284 presents a treatise focused on this topic.
The concept of differentiation serves __both as a structural and developmental term__, in which it clarifies the problems that must be solved at each stage of development (in the direction toward stronger differentiation). In contrast to older evolutionary theory, one dispenses with: a.) the thesis of causal determination of development;
b.) to the thesis of linearity and irreversibility;
c.) to the thesis of universal uniformity in the course of world history.
Through these concessions, but above all through the special position of the concept of social differentiation, it is possible to reconcile the old enemies evolutionism and functionalism.
