Durkheim → B2055 acknowledges scientific validity only to the extent that educational science is traceable to psychology and sociology as empirical sciences. In this respect, the focus is on the social fact of education, not on arbitrary practice (1966, pp. 59ff.). → B2055.
According to Durkheim → B2055 the sociological component comes to the fore to the extent that the educational system becomes the subject of social controversy. In educational systems that are socially uncontroversial, the only problems that arise are those that can be resolved at the psychological level—namely, questions of effective methods. If, on the other hand, goals become controversial, sociology becomes relevant to both goals and means; thus, 1966, p. 101 → B2055.
