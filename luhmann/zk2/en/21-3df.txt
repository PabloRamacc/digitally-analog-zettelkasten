Very frequently the characteristic is found in determinations of the system concept: that the associated units stand more in interaction with each other than with the environment. So, for example, Knox 1955, p. 133 → B1004, citing: C.P. Loomis / J.A. Beegle, Rural Social Systems, New York 1950, p. 5 → B1005.
See also Irle's remarks from 1963, pp. 117f. → B908, who points to the special situation at "external regions" (boundary sites) of a system that often interact more with the environment than with members of the system.
