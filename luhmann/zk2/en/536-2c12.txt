“Stress of Nonresponse” in Chapple-Sayles, p. 129ff. → B928 Implicitly, this is based on the concept of horror vacui, which, however, is not specifically elaborated upon. When people are together, communication must be constant. A lack of response is therefore a problem—both for the participants and for the situational system—especially for the latter, which threatens to fall apart if no one speaks; for the participants personally, perhaps only to the extent that they are committed to the situation.
One therefore responds to silence by taking the initiative, by stepping into the breach.
Reference in Chapple / Sayles, p. 130 → B928 on good negotiators who can take advantage of this situational pressure and let the others talk until they are exhausted: “they can outwait the other person.”
