The decisive reason against an empirical determination of problems is: that functional reference problems are not merely "open," more or less consciously experienced and detectable in experience, but also include solved ones. Functional analysis is interested in—and perhaps especially!—in solved problems. However, one cannot readily identify problems at their solutions: on the one hand, concrete solutions are usually multifunctional; on the other, the meaning of the problematic lies precisely in the multiple possibilities for solution. See also → 54/2c2a. This __broad__ concept of problem is closely linked to sociology's general tendency toward transcending the manifestly experienced; see → 21/12a.
