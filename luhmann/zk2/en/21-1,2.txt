und objective, social purpose clearly go back to Durkheim → B275, who for this reason introduces the term "fonction" into sociology. The subjective-finalistic (for Durkheim → B275: individualistic) explanation can never account for the unity and constancy of social facts. See also → 21/6ba.
p. 117 → B275: "__Quand donc on entreprend d'expliquer un phénomène social, il faut rechercher séparément la cause efficiente qui le produit et la fonction qu'il remplit__. We use the word function in preference to that of end or goal precisely because social phenomena generally do not exist for the useful results they produce. What must be determined is whether there is correspondence between the considered fact and the general needs of the social organism, and what this correspondence consists in, without concern as to whether it was intentional or not.
