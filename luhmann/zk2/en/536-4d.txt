The principle that interactions are guided by opportunities for consensus also applies in formal organizations; it is just that in such settings, there are more options available to substitute for consensus.
However, these alternative options are by no means the ideal, but merely a kind of last resort. It is therefore also misguided to condemn actions that focus primarily on the individuals and the price of their consent and to measure them against the ideal of a purely objective decision-making process. This ideal must be abandoned because it is not socially adequate (which, incidentally, presupposes the transition from the optimal model to the utility model).
