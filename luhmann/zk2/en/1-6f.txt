A characteristic feature of classical organizational theory—perhaps its defining principle—is that it explains the part-whole schema with the aid of the end-means schema. This is stated very explicitly by Kosiol, G., p. 15ff. → B41, but usually highly unclear—something like this (Schramm, p. 5 → B71): as “the essence of organization”: “1.) Parts are brought together to form a whole.
2.) This connection is purposeful and regulated to ensure the harmony of the whole.
3.) The parts that are united must be the most suitable ones available for the intended purpose.
4.) The union is formed for the purpose of joint achievement.
5.) The relationships between people, between people and objects, and between objects are regulated.
