At the level of interaction systems, there are hardly any possibilities for differentiating the system into subsystems for which the interaction system itself serves as the environment (though there are some initial examples, e.g., side games, whispered agreements).
Instead, increases in complexity arise through role differentiation, particularly through irreplaceability and asymmetries in the various roles involved, e.g., buyer/seller—in the monetary economy; teacher/student.
