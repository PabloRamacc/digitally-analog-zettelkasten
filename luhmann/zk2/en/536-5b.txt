Jones / Thibaut → B1734 propose a classification of interactions based on two different types of characteristics: I. According to the intensity of the interaction, i.e., the degree of mutual dependence of behavior. This idea takes on a more concrete form in that it renders the programming of one’s own behavior independent. This results in three types (p. 154ff. → B1734): 1.) “noncontingent interactions”;
2.) “asymmetrically contingent interactions”;
3.) “reciprocally contingent interactions.”
II. Based on the type of goals pursued by the participants: A. Facilitation of personal goal attainment: 1.) "Facilitation of personal goal attainment";
2.) “Securing motivational and value support”;
3.) “directly maximizing beneficent social response”;
