Satisfaction value and probability are relatively invariant against each other but not fully separable from one another.
Secure expectations can be disappointing; insecure ones may still denote a very desirable event.
Moreover, the increasingly ensured fulfillment of expectations is often, indeed almost typical, associated with a loss in satisfaction value; cf. → 532/1a1. Cf. also Stogdill, p. 61 → B321: "… in a series of action-satisfaction sequences, successive reinforcements of expectation appear to produce a diminishing effect upon expectation. That is, the initial satisfactions of an expectation are more effective in increasing expectation than later ones.
On the other hand, completely excluded expectations also have no value of satisfaction, and every non-fulfillment of expectations, even unsatisfying ones, is as such → 21/3a1pe5b1.
