From the __17th century__:
See Coustures 1687, p. 169ff.) → B3178; de Scuderi 1680, I, pp. 36–64 → B3175. Plaisir is given prior to all experience; thus, it is not an “ouvrage du temps,” but is originally connected to life (though contaminated by the Fall and self-love).
But then viewed positively after all: Coustures 1687, p. 175 → B3178[*]: “Pleasure gives man a second life; it snatches him from death; it banishes the gloomy mood of melancholy; it contributes to his health; it restores calm to his mind; and it helps him to live.” (still without explicit reference to social issues and formulated __as a counterpoint to__ an overly gloomy outlook on life
