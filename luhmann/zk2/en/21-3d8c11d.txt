__Jurisdiction__; see also → 21/3d8c17c
While jurisdiction holds a central position within the institutions of conflict resolution, its effectiveness is nevertheless limited.
Its function is primarily hindered by the fact that a.) law structures all conflicts such that exactly half of all litigants must lose (in other words, there is no possibility for compromises or ingeniously found solutions—at the expense of third parties)—and this is an institutional condition imposed on disputants by law for general reasons (and not merely those related to its function in case of conflict).
In addition—and without this final thought—Bredemeier 1962, p. 85 → B1618. See also → 3414/5c → 3414/5c11.
