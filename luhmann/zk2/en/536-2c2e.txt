A key feature of situational systems and their dynamics is that the behavior that creates or resolves problems __is__ always __behavior among those present__—and, more precisely, among those present who have already adapted their self-presentation to the situation. This shapes the problem, the motivational context, and the behavioral strategy in situational systems.
Whoever disrupts not only disturbs the social system but, at the same time, in the eyes of those affected, their personal self-presentation: “And he will find that his offense has been committed in the very presence of those who are offended by it. Those who break the rules of interaction commit their crimes in jail.” (Goffman 1957, p. 48 → B1764)
