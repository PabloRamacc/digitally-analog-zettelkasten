accepting one or another expression, the possibility of problem shifting rests. Tensions appear here and there, wander around in the organization, and thereby mediate between the various possible actions. In other words, follow-up problems that are not anticipatorily considered (planned) press themselves forward on their own and force their consideration.
p. 42f. → B144: "anything that happens in an organization implies both some reduction and some increase in tensions – some redistribution of the inventory of tensions."
This conception also includes conceptions about the process of transforming such unarticulated, perhaps unconscious tensions into actions – namely the decision-making process. Gore → B144 distinguishes 3 thresholds of this process: articulation of the problem, demand for collective attention (claim to attention) with attribution to a specific cause and sanctioning (in the sense of authorization) (p. 43f. → B144).
