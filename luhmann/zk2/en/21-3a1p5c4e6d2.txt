Misunderstanding requires no less than a sparring match of social gestures. The zealot who turns this sparring match into an actual battle, who takes a friendly initiation too seriously, is unlikely to be trusted with lighter comments on one's work or with doubts and misgivings; nor can he learn those parts of the working code which are communicated only by hint and gesture. He cannot be trusted because, though not fit for stratagems, he is suspected of being prone to treason. In order that men may communicate freely and confidentially, they must be able to take a good deal of each other's sentiments for granted. They must feel at ease about their silences as well as about their utterances.
