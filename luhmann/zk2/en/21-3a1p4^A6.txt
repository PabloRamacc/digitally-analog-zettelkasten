The coordination of attitudes and factual contexts through the identity of the symbol must no longer be understood in terms of a fixed correlation. If it were so: if an attitude corresponded to one thing, the symbol would be superfluous. Then one could work with invariant relations. Then there would be no freedom.
In fact, however, factual contexts and attitudes are relatively invariant toward each other. One can change one's attitude toward the matter or generalize one's attitude so that it remains intact despite changing factual relationships. Furthermore, a observer's thematic interest can also shift.
