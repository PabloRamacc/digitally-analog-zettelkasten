A fundamental prerequisite for the theory of involved systems is that they __abandon thinking in the ontological categories of part and whole__X. → 21/3d8db; → 21/3d8dd; → 21/3d8de. See also → 1/6; → 21/3d8da; → 21/3d8d(R); → 21/3d8dc.
applied to system relationships.
The starting point is that concrete action systems are never nested to such an extent that one completely subsumes the other; this assumption contradicts the system concept itself, which presupposes a functional autonomy, albeit limited.
One can, purely logically correct notwithstanding, adhere to the thought-schema Whole/Part. This then forces all involved systems – except for the starting system that is thematically treated as such – into an abstract perspective: Only one analytical aspect of the involved systems is part of the whole in this sense.
