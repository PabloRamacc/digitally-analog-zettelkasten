In stratified societies, the education system emphasizes moral and behavioral qualities over purely intellectual ones. Intellectual achievements are less reliably passed down in a way that secures social status than moral qualities are. Furthermore, with regard to morality, the educational program can be more easily adapted to a given status hierarchy than with regard to cognition. It is easier to make the moral qualities of the children of high-status individuals seem plausible (because they are already attributed to them in advance) than their intelligence.
