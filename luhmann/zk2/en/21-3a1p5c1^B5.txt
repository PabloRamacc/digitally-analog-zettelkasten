By claiming respect and recognition of one's self-interpretation, one simultaneously promises to honor behavioral expectations formulated on that basis. This constitutes the meaning of identity as temporal stability: one holds out the prospect of remaining faithful to one's social self, or: effectively being what one presents oneself as, in other words: not disappointing others.
Regarding this "moral" aspect of social identification, Goffman discusses various points elsewhere, e.g., pp. 6f. → B739 (p. 13) → B814[*] → B814: "Society is organized on the principle that any individual who possesses certain social characteristics has a moral right to expect that others will value and treat him in a correspondingly appropriate way.
