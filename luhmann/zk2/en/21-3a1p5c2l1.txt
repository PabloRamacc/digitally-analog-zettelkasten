At least in this application case, a connection with the distinction between emotional and functional stabilization is also recognizable: emotionally stabilized social orders must be more sensitive to deviations of real from expected feelings. Fundamentally on this point → 52/4b2 et seq.; → 522/3.
This also means that functionally stabilized social orders can tolerate a higher degree of such deviations, and thus also endure a higher degree of contradiction between symbolic usage (explicit self-presentation) and actual self-feeling.
