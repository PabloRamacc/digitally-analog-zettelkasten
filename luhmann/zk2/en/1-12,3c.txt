For a distinction between “normative and descriptive approaches to organizational research,” see Dill 1964, pp. 46ff. → B16, who, in this regard, assesses the current state of research and the prospects for the development of a unified organizational science as particularly unfavorable.
See also Wolin 1960, esp. pp. 402ff. → B158 with significant simplifications along the lines of calculative rationality / a warm-hearted human community; similar to Kerr / Fisher → B159.
For a distinction between the logical, descriptive, and normative uses of decision theory, see Gäfgen 1963, p. 50ff. → B160, who, after careful investigations—particularly into the descriptive applications—decides in favor of the normative conception.
See also Litterer 1965, p. 14ff., who makes this distinction. → B161 – which is decisive for the empirical method.
