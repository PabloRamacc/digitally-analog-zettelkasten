See also Goffman, p. 30ff. → B739 (p. 48 → B814)[*], p. 30 → B739: "I have suggested that a performer tends to conceal or underplay those activities, facts, and motives which are incompatible with an idealized version of himself and his products. In addition, a performer often engenders in his audience the belief that he is related to __them__ in a more ideal way than is always the case." (p. 30 → B739)
Goffman, p. 31f. → B739 (p. 48/49 → B814)[*]discusses two examples that merge into one another: accentuating a performance as particularly important or irreplicable, or, when routine becomes transparent, as the only and essential routine. Full validity is claimed for each respective performance; it possesses something special that satisfies the audience's need for individuality or the role partner's needs. Salespeople, doctors, civil servants, pastors create the impression that this particular case lies especially close to their hearts: the bed-side-manner (p. 32 → B739) of doctors and so on.
Technically, this idealization presupposes a high degree of "audience segregation" based on uniqueness or at least preferential treatment Goffman, p. 31, 83 → B739.
