Task analysis is abstract insofar as it disregards the specific individuals who are to carry out the task.
Task analysis views the overall task of an enterprise as complex and identifies the individual or sub-tasks contained within it as “components” (p. 29ff. → B41). In this context, the guiding concepts used are not so much the “end-means” concepts (which would require greater precision) as the concepts of “whole” and “part” (see “subtask”). See also → 1/6 .
