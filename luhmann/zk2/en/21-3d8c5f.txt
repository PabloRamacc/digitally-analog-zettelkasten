The possibility of excluding behavior expectations that cannot be physically fulfilled rests on the fact that they practically do not occur. No one will "expect" a friend whom he knows at the other end of the world to dinner; no one will "expect" physical labor performance that clearly exceeds all human capacities. Of course, there are mistakes, for example regarding the financial capabilities (including creditworthiness) of others. But even here, the behavior expectation is actually more about timely disclosure of facts than about impossibilities. Social sciences can therefore treat physical and biological obstacles as well as social role conflicts as surmountable difficulties.
