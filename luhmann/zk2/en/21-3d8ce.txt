→ 21/3d8c This broad concept of conflict, which may need to be replaced terminologically by another designation (e.g., state of conflict, functional contradiction, tension [strain]), does not necessarily imply open conflict behavior (dispute) per se. For now, a distinction between conflict and dispute shall be maintained in this sense.
This differentiation is especially necessary because the dispute has very specific – namely adaptive – functions, which cannot generally be said about conflict as such. See → 21/3d8c12.
The distinction between conflict and dispute also enables treating the absence of dispute as problematic – and not, in the sense of old teachings on harmony, as happiness. For this see esp. Coser, p. 81ff. → B1501
