Practical consequences of this analysis: 1.) Deciding must attach itself to evils, to problems that have arisen, and not to preconceived ideals, because with the latter value problems are easier to solve.
This proposal cannot be justified absolutely but only conditionally; it holds only if rational action is intended under the given circumstances.
2.) Relatively fixed value relations are not presupposed as conditions of correctness in deciding; they emerge as a by-product of decision-making: so to speak, expected as statistics of past series of decisions. So (without this last thought) Braybrooke - Lindblom, p. 87 → B509.
