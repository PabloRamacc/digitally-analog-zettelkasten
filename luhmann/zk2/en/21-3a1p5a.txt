__Social identifications have a function for expectation formation__. They enable a connection between signs and symbols with behavioral expectations, thus enabling the anticipation of others' behavior and the projection of one's own, expected-by-others lines of action in __a single__ coordinated context.
The use of such identities in social interaction thereby simultaneously defines a possibility of consensus and an opportunity for mutual reinforcement of expectations. One projects their social self, their role, their situational definition into the situation and thus provides others with a basis for behavior. In this sense Goffman, p. 2 → B739 speaks of the "promissory character" of action (and perhaps also: of self-presentation) by an individual.
similarly Gross - Stone 1964, e.g., p. 1 → B621: "...the maintenance of one's identity assists the maintenance of the other."
