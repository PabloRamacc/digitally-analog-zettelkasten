p. 47 → B776In the terminology of learning theory, these universalistic and particularistic modes of association of objects are two different 'gradients of generalization'. The actor extends his attention, and includes in the same class as objects of his attention and motivation, objects which '**belong together**' by virtue of some criterion. He tends to treat objects which, by the relevant criterion, do belong together in the same or similar ways. What the concepts of universalism and particularism do is to distinguish two different types of criterion by which generalization may take place from the point of view of a given actor.
